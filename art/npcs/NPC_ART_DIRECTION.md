# Aergyle NPC art direction

Use this brief for every newly generated civilian NPC and skill mentor. The
purpose is to create a populated world of specific, credible people—not a
catalogue of attractive adventurer portraits.

## Non-negotiable character rules

- NPCs are adults by default. Do not use childlike facial proportions, teenage
  styling, or a youthful fashion-editorial presentation unless a separate,
  explicitly approved child-NPC brief exists.
- When the design specifies a woman or a man, make the intended adult identity
  immediately readable in the completed asset through the mature face, body
  scale, grooming, civilian silhouette and tailored clothing. State the
  intended adult gender positively in the prompt and explicitly exclude child,
  teen and unintended gender-ambiguous presentation. Do not depend on one
  prop, hairstyle, or sexualised clothing to communicate identity.
- Write the character before writing the clothes: give every NPC a role,
  temperament, lived experience, one habit, and at least two visible story
  details that follow from those facts.
- The profession must be legible from the tools, wear patterns, posture and
  working kit. Avoid generic adventurer clothing, combat stances and decorative
  fantasy props.

## Personality over attractiveness

Do not make conventional beauty, youth, clean skin, perfect symmetry, happiness
or fashionable clothing the default. A character may be handsome, beautiful or
well-presented only when that is an intentional part of the authored role—not
the generator's fallback.

Across a batch, deliberately vary:

- age: young adults through elders;
- build, height and posture: compact, lanky, stooped, broad, wiry, heavyset,
  braced or otherwise role-appropriate;
- facial structure and condition: broad or broken noses, uneven brows, scars,
  sun, soot, wind, wrinkles, missing teeth only where readable and appropriate,
  and other non-glamorous signs of life;
- hair, facial hair and grooming: cropped, braided, thinning, greying, shaved,
  tangled, neat, beard, clean-shaven and mixed styles;
- expression and disposition: amused, guarded, patient, focused, stern,
  tired, proud, distracted, practical or quietly warm;
- clothes, repairs, materials, palette, social status and carried equipment.

Avoid repeating a batch-wide template of smooth-faced, athletic people in clean
layered leather. No pin-up, fashion-model, romance-cover, or "heroic default"
look. An unglamorous character must still be dignified and specific; visual wear
is storytelling, never a joke.

## Profession direction

Every profession needs an adult practitioner who visibly understands the work.
Choose the character's physical capability, dress and tools from the task—not
from fashion or a generic adventurer template.

- **Blacksmiths and weaponsmiths:** portray serious, dedicated master artisans:
  a credible work-built body for their individual build, soot or heat wear,
  scarred or calloused hands where visible, maintained tools, practical heavy
  clothing and a composed, experienced expression. They are never presented as
  weak, novice, dull or clean-costumed warriors. This requirement applies to
  every gender; professions are not restricted by gender.
- **Woodcutters:** show strength, risk awareness and long practice—sound axe
  handling, timber wedges, measuring cord, repaired outdoor kit and a calm,
  non-combat stance.
- **Gatherers and foragers:** favour observation, organisation and fieldcraft:
  weatherproof layers, sample pouches, baskets with a limited readable harvest,
  practical knives in sheaths and a thoughtful working action.
- **Alchemists:** show careful experimentation and portable craft tools rather
  than wizard robes or supernatural effects. Stains, marked tools and a
  controlled testing pose are more useful than magic.

## Prompt and asset checklist

1. Start with `one adult <man/woman/person>, age range, profession` and include
   the intended identity, temperament and work history.
2. Name at least three non-idealised visual anchors: for example a crooked nose,
   a repaired garment, uneven haircut, stiff knee, sun weathering, distinct
   build or work-worn hands. Do not reuse the same anchors across the batch.
3. Name a non-combat action and a limited set of profession-specific tools.
4. Require a complete 2:3 full-body portrait with both feet and gear visible,
   a transparent RGBA background, no scenery, no floor and no cast-shadow
   stage. The runtime export is 512×768 PNG from the 1024×1536 master.
5. Explicitly exclude child, teen, glamour-model, pin-up, sexualised clothing,
   glossy 3D, anime, chibi, generic adventurer armour, text, logos, watermarks
   and extra characters. Add any role-specific exclusions.
6. Review the result at full size and at runtime size. Reject it if it reads as
   too young, an unintended identity, a duplicate silhouette, a fashion model,
   or a professionless adventurer. Document the accepted design and prompt.
