Balance design · vocation efficiency implemented, the rest is proposal

Where X/(X+K) belongs in Aergyle

A review of every stat the game computes, traced from its source to what the player sees, with a verdict on whether a saturation curve would help it, hurt it, or is already there.

Based on the repository as of 23 September 2026: src/, prisma/ content packs, seeds and docs/. Balance numbers come from content packs in the repo, not from the live database. Vocation efficiency has since been implemented as described in its section.

Key findings

  1. The formula is already live. Armor and magic resist use 100 / (100 + armor) as a damage multiplier (src/server/combat/rules.ts:50), which is exactly reduction = armor / (armor + 100). K is hard-coded at 100, and the same formula applies when you hit monsters and when monsters hit you. For armor, the open question is what K should be, not whether to use the curve.
  2. One system had the opposite problem, and it's now fixed. Vocation efficiency used time × (1 − efficiency/100), so each point was worth more than the last: 50 gave ×2 output, 90 gave ×10, and 100 hit a 1-second floor. It now uses time × 100 / (100 + efficiency), the same shape as travel, so each point adds the same output.
  3. The shared find/quantity modifier adds sources linearly and then hard-clamps them. Skill level alone reaches the +150% find cap at skill 301, after which Luck and Efficiency gear do nothing. It isn't urgent yet (hunting content tops out around skill 66), but it will become a problem as players reach higher skill levels.
  4. For reductions, X/(X+K) doesn't actually give diminishing returns. On damage taken or time spent, it makes every point worth the same amount of survivability or output. It is only truly diminishing on chances and bonuses. This decides where it belongs, and it's explained in the next section.
  5. Many of the listed systems don't exist or aren't read. Accuracy, Lifesteal, Thorns, Mana, Prayer, Gold Find and the Experience Gain stat are defined and appear on gear, but no gameplay code reads them. There are no cooldowns, crafting success or crafting quality systems. They need no curve yet.
  6. Two non-curve problems came up that matter more than any curve. Weapon attack speed was added to the character's base speed and scaled by rarity; that's now fixed. Three routes let any signed-in player grant themselves XP, stackable XP multipliers or items. See side findings.

1Read this first: what a percentage actually buys

Whether X/(X+K) is diminishing depends on what the resulting percentage does. The game has three kinds of scaling stats, and the curve behaves differently for each.

Reductions of a cost: damage taken, time spent

If one point of a stat removes one percentage point of damage or time (as resistances and evasion do, and vocation efficiency did until its fix), the value of each point grows. Going from 0 to 10% cuts damage by 10%, but going from 80 to 90% halves it. Survivability or output is 1 / (1 − p), which is infinite at 100%. Applying X/(X+K) to the reduction gives 1 + X/K, which is a straight line. Each point adds the same survivability or output, and the displayed percentage can never reach 100%. This is where the curve belongs. Armor already works this way, and so do travel (base × 100 / speed is the same formula) and now vocation efficiency.

Chances and bonuses on a gain: find chance, quantity, crit chance

A linear percentage already has a linear value here: +10% find is worth the same at any point. Applying X/(X+K) makes each point genuinely worth less. That only helps when sources pile into a hard cap and later points become worthless, or when several sources multiply together.

Flat amounts: health, damage, regen, inventory slots

These are linear by nature and players read them directly. A curve would only make upgrades feel weaker. Keep them linear.

The reference points you listed, including what they mean when the percentage is a reduction:

So on reductions the curve prevents two broken states (a 100% reduction and accelerating value per point), but it doesn't stop stat inflation. Survivability still grows linearly with the rating. Inflation is controlled by how K relates to the ratings content hands out, which is why most of the armor section is about K.

2How a stat reaches gameplay today

Every system shares one aggregation pipeline, described in docs/CHARACTER_STATS_SYSTEM.md. Knowing it tells you where a curve can go.

  1. Base valueCharacterStatGrowth rule per stat: level-1 value + min(cap, perLevel × (level − 1)), with defaults in DEFAULT_LEVEL_GROWTH (src/utils/stats.ts:118). Chance stats have a capped growth. Armor, health and damage grow forever. Economy and vocation stats don't grow. Per-character CharacterBaseStat rows are added on top, but nothing writes them yet.
  2. EquipmentresolveEffectiveItemStats (src/utils/itemInstanceStats.ts:77): ItemStat + unlocked ItemStatProgression + ToolEfficiency → × RarityConfig.statMultiplier (Worthless 0.5 … Common 1.0 … Epic 1.7 … Divine 3.0) or a per-item override → capped by ItemStat.maxValue → + UserItemStatModifier.
  3. Temporary effectsOne active food, potion or elixir per character (userActiveFoodEffect.upsert, src/server/food-effects/service.ts:93). Its FoodEffectStat rows are flat.
  4. AggregationgetCharacterStatSnapshot (src/server/stats.ts:85) sums base + equipment + effect with combineStatRecords. Every source is flat and additive. There are no percentage modifiers anywhere yet.
  5. Caps and floorscalculateFinalStatsFromTotals (src/utils/stats.ts:213): crit chance ≤ 100, evasion/block ≤ 75, resistances between −100 and 75, attack speed ≥ 0.1, and most others ≥ 0. Armor, damage and health are uncapped.
  6. Activity snapshotTaken at start: hunting uses scalar columns (armorSnapshot, luckSnapshot …) plus a riskConfig JSON; dungeons use a combatSnapshot JSON; gathering uses scalar columns; vocations use unitSeconds; travel uses endsAt.
  7. Gameplay rulePure resolvers at claim time: src/server/combat/rules.ts, src/server/dungeons/resolver.ts, src/server/hunting/resolver.ts, src/server/expeditions/rewards.ts, src/game/world/travel.ts. The first four also run in the browser in the admin simulators.
  8. DisplayRaw ratings only. No converted percentage for armor, find bonus or efficiency is shown anywhere.

Because every source is flat and summed, the stacking order today is just "sum, then cap". Any curve goes between the final total (step 5) and the gameplay rule (step 7), and it has to be applied to the snapshotted value, not the live one.

3Strong candidates

Vocation efficiency → action time

Implemented23 September 2026Woodcutting · Mining · Fishing

Action time now uses X/(X+K) on time saved, so output grows linearly with efficiency. It no longer accelerates towards a 1-second floor.

How it works now

  1. Base valueVocationalResource.defaultSeconds per resource (9 to 120 s in the content packs). The efficiency base stat is 0 and doesn't grow with level.
  2. EquipmentEach tool's ToolEfficiency.baseEfficiency becomes the WOODCUTTING/MINING/FISHING_EFFICIENCY stat, multiplied by rarity (up to ×3.0). Any other equipped item with the stat also adds to it, as does a food effect.
  3. AggregationgetToolEfficiencyForAction reads snapshot.totals[stat]. clampEfficiency now only floors it at 0. The old upper cap of 100 is gone, because the new curve never reaches zero time (src/server/vocations/tools.ts).
  4. Gameplay rulecomputeEffectiveUnitSeconds in src/game/vocationStats.ts: unitSeconds = max(1, round(base × 100 / (100 + e))). The 100 is the constant VOCATION_EFFICIENCY_HALF_TIME. tools.ts re-exports the function, so the start service, the resources route and the skills page call it unchanged.
  5. SnapshotStored as UserVocationalActivity.unitSeconds at start. Activities that were already running keep the speed they started with. The realtime daemon settles one unit each time unitSeconds elapses.
  6. DisplayUnchanged. The skills page shows only the time an action takes. There's no base time and no formula.

Before and after

The old rule, max(1, floor(base × (1 − e/100))) with efficiency capped at 100, made output 1 / (1 − e/100), so every point was worth more than the one before:

EfficiencyBefore: timeBefore: outputNow: timeNow: output
1090%×1.1190.9%×1.10
2575%×1.3380%×1.25
5050%×266.7%×1.5

Rarity made the old extremes reachable. A tool with baseEfficiency 30 was 90% at Divine, ten times the output, and 34 or above hit the cap and the 1-second floor. Every unit is a daemon settlement, so 1-second units meant one database transaction per player per second on the Pi. Both risks are gone.

Choices made

  • X is the final efficiency total from the snapshot, totals[efficiencyStat]: tool, other gear and food, after the rarity multiplier and maxValue. Floored at 0, with no upper cap.
  • K = 100, the efficiency that halves the time. It's the same shape as travel, where +100 speed halves a journey. It's a code constant rather than an /admin setting, which keeps the change to one function with no table or migration. To keep a particular tool at its old speed, set it to 100 − that tool's efficiency.
  • Not level-dependent, because resources are already gated by vocation level.
  • Rounding is now round, as in applyMovementSpeed. The old floor gave a small hidden bonus on short actions.

Stacking order

// flat sources, summed exactly as before e = tool baseEfficiency × rarity + other gear efficiency × rarity + food effect // future "+x% efficiency" bonuses would multiply e here, before the curve unitSeconds = max(1, round(defaultSeconds × 100 / (100 + e))) // snapshot at start → daemon ticks

Player-facing

Unchanged. Players see the time a resource takes to gather and nothing else. From their side output is linear, so there's no diminishing return to explain.

Tests and docs

tests/vocation-speed.test.ts runs with npm run test:vocations. The formula is also noted in docs/VOCATIONS_SYSTEM.md.

Tradeoff

High-efficiency tools are slower than before: 50 efficiency now gives ×1.5 output instead of ×2. The repo's content packs author no Woodcutting, Mining or Fishing efficiency. The only ToolEfficiency row (12, on the Reedwind Bow in scripts/seedHunting.ts) is for Hunting, which uses efficiency for finds and quantity, not time. Live tool values weren't checked.

Armor and magic resist → damage reduction

Strong candidate: change K, the curve is already therePriority 1Dungeons · Hunting retaliation

The formula is already in place with K = 100 hard-coded. With the gear already authored, a level-50 Epic set gives 86% physical reduction, and every future tier pushes this closer to immunity.

Current implementation

  1. Base valueArmor 0 + 0.5 per level, uncapped. Magic resist 0 + 0.3 per level, uncapped.
  2. EquipmentFlat armor per piece × rarity. Trailwarden (level 1) totals 44 base armor across 8 pieces, and Duskwarden (level 50) totals 340 base armor and 36 base magic resist. One dish gives +5 magic resist.
  3. Capsarmor = max(0, total), with no ceiling.
  4. SnapshotHunting: armorSnapshot/magicResistSnapshot columns. Dungeons: the combatSnapshot JSON, built by combatSnapshotFromStats.
  5. Gameplay ruleresolveCreatureStrike (rules.ts:83): physical × protectionMultiplier(armor) + magic × protectionMultiplier(magicResist) + elemental × (1 − resist%), then × crit, then × 0.5 on block. protectionMultiplier(p) = 100 / (100 + max(0, p)).
  6. Reverse directioncharacterStrike (dungeons/resolver.ts:145) applies the same function to Creature.armor/magicResist when the character attacks. The Goblin has 2 armor.
  7. DisplayRaw only: "Armor / magic resist" in the dungeon planner (DungeonExplorer.tsx:760). The armor row is commented out on the character sheet (CharacterStats.tsx:233). The formula is documented in docs/DUNGEONS_SYSTEM.md.

Would diminishing returns make sense?

Yes, and it already does. The curve is the right family for mitigation. The problem is that K is fixed, while the two authored sets, 49 levels apart, differ almost eightfold in base armor (44 vs 340):

CharacterArmorToday (K = 100)Level-scaled K (example)
Level 1, Trailwarden set (Rare ×1.35)5937.3%44.2% (K = 75)
Level 50, still in Trailwarden8445.6%11.2% (K = 663)
Level 50, Duskwarden set (Epic ×1.7)60385.8%47.6% (K = 663)
Level 50, Duskwarden set (Divine ×3.0)1,04591.3%61.2% (K = 663)
Level 300, no gear (base growth only)15059.9%3.9% (K = 3,663)

At level 50 with today's K, the Epic set takes 14% of physical damage and the Rare set takes 54%, a ×3.8 gap. Creature damage is a flat authored range, so any creature built to threaten the Epic player will be very hard on the Rare player at the same level. Hunting hides this with its 30% health-loss budget. Dungeons don't, and only a level-1 Goblin exists so far. Settle this before authoring higher-level dungeons.

What X should be

The final armor rating: finalStats.armor after all flat sources and any future percentage bonuses or debuffs, floored at 0. Not equipment armor alone, because base armor also grows forever (level 300 alone is 150) and has to go through the same conversion. Magic resist works the same way with its own K.

What K should be

  • Global fixed (today). Simplest, but only works if each gear tier adds little armor. The authored sets don't.
  • Attacker-level-dependent (recommended): K = K₀ + K₁ × attackerLevel. On-level gear stays near a target reduction at every level, and old gear's percentage falls as content levels rise. This is the standard MMO approach.
  • Per stat: yes. Armor and magic resist grow at different rates (340 vs 36 base on Duskwarden), so each gets its own K₀/K₁.
  • Where the attacker level comes from. Creature has no level field. What already exists: in dungeons, max(dungeon.requiredLevel, location.requiredLevel), which startDungeonRun already computes (service.ts:507). In hunting, the location's requiredLevel (requiredHuntingLevel is a skill level on a different scale). When the character attacks a monster, the character's level. A Creature.level column would only be needed if one creature should hit differently in different places.
  • How to pick numbers. Choose the target reduction r for on-level, on-tier, full-set gear. Then K(L) = expectedArmor(L) × (1 − r) / r. With the two authored sets and r ≈ 45–48%, that gives K ≈ 75 at level 1 and ≈ 663 at level 50, so roughly 75 + 12 × (level − 1). This is an illustration from the content, not a number to ship.
  • Authoring aid. The creature editor can show the armor a monster needs for r% against on-level characters: K(L) × r / (1 − r).

Where the formula goes

Keep it in src/server/combat/rules.ts, which is pure and imported by the in-browser admin simulators. Give protectionMultiplier(protection, k) an explicit K and add armorConstant(level, curve). Resolve K when the run or hunt starts and store it in combatSnapshot / riskConfig, both JSON so no migration is needed. A missing key means K = 100, so runs already in flight resolve exactly as they started.

Stacking order

base armor (level rule) + flat gear armor (× rarity, ≤ maxValue, + instance modifiers) + flat food/potion armor → × (1 + Σ "% armor" bonuses) // none exist yet; would go here → − armor shred / debuffs, floor 0 // rating level, never on the % = final armor rating A // snapshotted at start K = K₀ + K₁ × attacker level // resolved at start, snapshotted reduction = A / (A + K) physical hit × (1 − reduction) × crit × (block ? 0.5 : 1)

Never add a "+x% damage reduction" item directly to the converted percentage, because that brings back linear stacking. If such a stat appears, multiply it as a separate layer: (1 − rarmor)(1 − rother).

Player-facing

Show both, in context. Dungeon planner: "Armor 603 · 48% physical reduction here" (against that dungeon's level, where the number matters). Character sheet tooltip: "Armor 603: 48% against level-50 enemies. Each point adds the same survivability. The percentage rises more slowly as armor grows and against stronger enemies."

Tradeoff

With a level-scaled K, armor from old gear loses percentage as players level. That's intended in most MMOs, but it is a visible nerf to anyone staying in old gear, and every monster's armor has to be authored on the new scale.

Expedition find and quantity bonuses

Strong candidate, lower urgencyPriority 2Gathering · Hunting · Dungeon loot (Luck)

Linear sources summed into hard clamps. Skill level alone eventually fills the cap and makes Luck and Efficiency gear worthless.

Current implementation

  1. SourcesVocation skill level (grows with play). Luck: base 0 and no level growth, from gear and food (+5 dishes). Gathering/Hunting Efficiency: tailored gear +2 to +5 and tools, × rarity, plus food. All snapshotted at departure.
  2. Gameplay rulecalculateExpeditionRewardModifiers (src/server/expeditions/rewards.ts:16) clamps luck to −100…250 and efficiency to 0…250, then:
    find% = clamp(0.5·(skill−1) + 0.4·luck + efficiency, −50, 150)
    quantity% = clamp(0.2·(skill−1) + 0.1·max(0, luck) + 0.5·efficiency, 0, 100)
  3. Per dropcalculateExpeditionEffectiveFindChance: chance = clamp(base × (1 + find%), 0.001, 0.95). Quantity: roll × durationMultiplier × (1 + quantity%).
  4. ConsumersGathering (calculateGatheringRewards), Hunting and dungeons (rollCreatureDrops). Dungeons pass skill 1 and efficiency 0, so only Luck counts.
  5. DisplayThe reports store modifiers. The Hunting and Gathering screens show Luck and Efficiency with their sources. The admin simulators run the same function in the browser.

Would diminishing returns make sense?

Yes, for three reasons found in the code:

  • Dead stats at the cap. Once the sum passes 150, each extra point of Luck or Efficiency is worth nothing, and nothing tells the player.
  • Skill fills the cap by itself. Find from skill is 0.5 per level: +49.5% at skill 100, +99.5% at 200, and the full +150% at skill 301. The XP table in docs/ runs to level 353.
  • It double-counts progression. CHARACTER_STATS_SYSTEM.md keeps economy stats from growing with level because higher levels already unlock better content. Skill already does that here too, through CreatureDrop.requiredLevel and resource requiredSkillLevel.

It isn't urgent: hunting content peaks around skill 66, which is +32.5% from skill.

What X should be

The weighted rating before the clamp, keeping today's weights because they already define one: Xfind = 0.5·(skill−1) + 0.4·luck + efficiency, and the same for quantity with its own weights. Keep skill inside X, because that's what makes the curve rein it in. Negative X (luck below zero) stays linear with the existing −50 floor, since X/(X+K) misbehaves near X = −K.

What K should be, and why not a plain X/(X+K)

This is where the proposed formula needs adjusting. Any curve that starts at today's slope of 1 sits below today's line everywhere, so a plain M·X/(X+K) either cuts mid-game values (M = K = 150 gives +60% at X = 100, where today gives +100%) or boosts early-game ones (M = 200, K = 100 gives +40% at X = 25, where today gives +25%). Early drop tables are tuned against today's line, so neither is free.

Recommendation: keep it linear up to a knee, then apply X/(X+K) to the part above it.

bonus = X if X ≤ knee bonus = knee + (M − knee) · E / (E + K), E = X − knee otherwise K = M − knee // keeps the slope continuous: no visible kink at the knee

That's two admin numbers per modifier (knee and ceiling M), global, with no level or region dependence, because skill is already in X and drop tables are already regional. Players below the knee, which covers everyone today, see exactly the same numbers.

Where the formula goes

Keep src/server/expeditions/rewards.ts as the single home, taking the curve parameters as an argument, since the admin GatheringSimulator and HuntingAdminClient run it in the browser. Snapshot the parameters: hunting in riskConfig, dungeons in combatSnapshot. Gathering has no config JSON, so it needs one nullable JSON column, where null means today's formula for expeditions already under way.

Stacking order

X = 0.5·(skill − 1) + 0.4·luck + efficiency // luck and efficiency already include gear × rarity and food find% = X < 0 ? max(−50, X) : softCap(X, knee, M) chance per drop = f(base chance, find%) // see "drop chance conversion" below quantity = roll × duration quantityMultiplier × (1 + quantity%) // duration multiplier stays outside the curve

Player-facing

Show the resulting bonus: "Find bonus +96%", with a tooltip: "Skill, Luck and Efficiency add up to a find rating of 100. Above 75, each point adds a little less." Only mention the curve when it's actually in effect. Also fix Luck's description, which currently reads "Increases item drop quality". It actually raises find chance and quantity.

4Possible later candidates

Drop chance conversion

Possible: a different curve

Today: min(0.95, base × (1 + find%)). Most hunting drops are authored at 0.72–0.95 base, so even small find bonuses push them into the 0.95 clamp, and only rare drops really benefit.

X/(X+K) is the wrong tool here. The natural curve for a probability treats the bonus as extra attempts: chance = 1 − (1 − base)1 + find. Rare drops are nearly unchanged, and common drops approach certainty smoothly instead of hitting a clamp.

It lowers common-drop yields at high bonuses, so it's a deliberate reward decision rather than a free fix. Revisit it together with the knee curve.

Evasion, paired with Accuracy

Possible later

Today: flat percentage per attack style. Base 5 + 0.1 per level (growth capped at +10), gear × rarity, capped at 75%. Monsters have a flat evasion percentage. Accuracy is on gear (Trailwarden gloves +2, Duskwarden +8, a Wayfarer accessory +3) and grows with level, but no resolver reads it.

This is the natural place for a contested curve: evade chance = E / (E + c · attacker accuracy), where K is the attacker's accuracy. Accuracy would get a purpose, and rarity-scaled evasion couldn't reach silly values. But it changes the meaning of every authored evasion value from a percentage to a rating, and creatures would need accuracy. Do it when combat content grows. Realistic evasion today is about 20% at level 50, far from the cap.

Stacked defensive layers

Watch

Evasion (≤ 75%), block (≤ 75%, halves the hit, so at most 37.5% expected), armor (uncapped percentage) and elemental resistance (≤ 75%) multiply. Each layer is capped on its own, but together they compound.

Worst case today: 75% evasion × 75% block × Divine Duskwarden armor at K = 100 lets through 1.4% of physical damage, which is 73 times the survivability. A realistic level-50 Epic character (22% evasion, no block, 86% armor) takes 11%. Layering is not a curve target in itself. It's the reason to fix armor's K first and to keep the evasion and block caps under review when shields and evasion gear expand.

Gold Find

Possible laterNot read yet

Defined and formatted as a percentage, but nothing reads it: creatures don't drop gold, and settlements pay fixed quest rewards.

If it gets wired in, it becomes the most inflation-sensitive stat in the game, because gold is the only currency. Start it with a low hard cap or the same knee curve used for find, decided when the gold sources are designed.

5Keep as-is

A curve on these would add complexity or make upgrades feel worse, without fixing a real problem.

Elemental resistances

Linear + hard cap

Fire, Cold, Lightning and Poison are flat percentages clamped between −100 and 75 (stats.ts:241), applied as × (1 − resist/100). They're authored directly as percentages (gear 8–15, a dish +10, level growth up to +10). The 75 cap already rules out immunity and limits the value to ×4. A readable hard cap on resistances is the genre norm. Convert to a rating only if resistance gear becomes common enough to sit at the cap.

Block

Linear + hard cap

A flat percentage capped at 75 that halves damage, so the expected reduction is at most 37.5%. Bounded and easy to read. Only the layering note above applies.

Critical chance

Linear

A flat percentage clamped at 100, which is a natural cap (you can't crit more than always). Base 5 + up to +10 from level, with gear on top. Each point adds the same expected damage. A curve would only make crit gear feel weak. The thing to watch is rarity tripling crit chance on items; limit that per item with maxValue.

Critical damage

Linear

A multiplier of critDamage / 100 (base 150, +0.5 per level up to +50). It's worth crit chance × points, so the crit chance cap already bounds it.

Attack speed

LinearStacking fixed

Attacks per dungeon round, clamped between 0.1 and 10. A curve isn't the answer. The problem was that weapon speed was added to base speed and scaled by rarity, and that's now fixed (see side findings). Speed on other gear stays a flat bonus.

Health and damage

Linear

Flat, uncapped, and read directly by players. Level growth is flat per level on purpose, so each level is a smaller share of the total.

Health regeneration

Linear

Flat health per second (healthMath.ts), growing in proportion to health so a full heal takes equally long at every level. It only affects downtime between activities, not combat. A flat +2 food bonus is a big boost at level 1 and fades naturally as base regen grows.

Movement speed and travel

Already the right curve

travelSeconds = base × 100 / speed, with speed floored at 10 (src/game/world/travel.ts:22). Time saved is bonus / (bonus + 100), so it's already the X/(X+K) family with linear journeys per hour. Vocation efficiency now uses the same shape.

Hunting mishaps and movement speed

Probably unnecessary

clamp(1 − (speed − 100) × 0.005, 0.5, 1.5) (hunting/resolver.ts:234), linear with a clamp. If you ever touch it, 100 / speed gives the same 0.5 at speed 200 without the clamp and matches travel.

Carrying capacity

Linear

25 + floor(total) slots, uncapped (src/utils/inventoryCapacity.ts). Slots are discrete, readable and satisfying to upgrade. Add a hard cap only for UI or performance reasons.

XP bonuses

LinearFix the stacking order

The EXPERIENCE_GAIN stat (base 100) isn't read. XpMultiplier rows multiply when stackable (combineMultipliers, leveling.ts:218): five ×1.5 boosts give ×7.6. The settlements roadmap plans event XP bonuses on top. Sum the bonuses instead (1 + Σ(m − 1)) rather than adding a curve. If EXPERIENCE_GAIN is ever wired in, remember that its base is 100, meaning ×1.

Food, potion and elixir buffs

Already non-stacking

One active effect per character (an upsert), and a new one replaces the old. The single-slot design already solves buff stacking. Any Luck or Efficiency a buff gives passes through the find curve automatically, because it's inside X.

Base stat growth by level

Already piecewise

min(cap, perLevel × (level − 1)): chance stats are capped, while health, damage and armor are uncapped. It's a considered, admin-tunable design. Armor's uncapped growth is handled by a level-scaled K, not by changing this rule.

Rarity multipliers

Not a curve target

×0.5 to ×3.0 applies to every stat, percentage stats included. This is where inflation comes from, but the controls already exist per item: ItemStat.maxValue and StatRarityOverride (MULTIPLIER/ABSOLUTE). Rating stats can scale freely once their conversion saturates. Percentage stats should be capped per item.

Decide the curve for each of these when the system is designed. That's also when its UI copy gets written.

7Architecture proposal

One small pure primitive, with system-specific wrappers living where each rule already lives. No stat-curve registry and no per-stat config table: the codebase keeps rules close to their systems, and only three systems need a curve.

The primitive

// src/game/balance/curves.ts: pure, client-safe (src/game already holds shared pure rules) saturation(x, k) → x ≤ 0 ? 0 : x / (x + k) softCap(x, knee, ceiling) → x ≤ knee ? x : knee + (ceiling − knee) · saturation(x − knee, ceiling − knee)

Where K lives

Follow the existing singleton pattern (HuntingConfig, DungeonConfig, TravelConfig): one row with id 1, code defaults that reproduce today's behavior when no row exists, and editing in /admin. That's eight numbers in total: armor K₀/K₁, magic resist K₀/K₁, and find/quantity knee and ceiling. Vocation efficiency's K is a code constant for now (VOCATION_EFFICIENCY_HALF_TIME). It could join this row if it ever needs changing without a deploy. The natural home is /admin/character-stats, which already previews levels 48 and 50 side by side. It could show "on-level full set: 47% reduction" next to those columns.

Server and client

  • Server is authoritative: curve parameters are resolved at activity start, snapshotted, and applied at claim time by the pure resolvers.
  • The client reuses the same functions for previews and tooltips, with parameters passed in page data. It never recomputes an outcome.
  • Keep raw ratings in ComputedStats. The converted percentage depends on context (attacker level, activity), so it's computed where it's used and returned in page data and reports. Don't store it on the character.
  • Persistence: item data doesn't change, because ratings stay what gear authors wrote. New pieces are the config row plus curve data in activity snapshots (JSON keys, and one nullable column for gathering). A snapshot without curve data resolves with today's constants.
  • Tests: the node:test style in tests/travel.test.ts covers this: curve values at 0, K and 9K, negative input, the knee's continuity, and resolver outcomes with a fixed K.

9The three groups

Strongest candidates

  • Vocation efficiency → action time. Implemented Output is now linear in efficiency, with no 1-second ticks.
  • Armor and magic resist K. The curve exists, but a fixed K lets each gear tier approach immunity.
  • Find and quantity bonuses. Hard caps that skill fills on its own, leaving gear stats dead.

Possible later

  • Drop chance conversion, using the extra-attempts curve rather than X/(X+K).
  • Evasion vs Accuracy, a contested curve once creatures have accuracy.
  • Gold Find, once it's read.
  • Defense layering: review the evasion and block caps when shields expand.

Keep as-is

  • Elemental resistances and block (linear with a hard cap)
  • Crit chance and crit damage, health, damage, regen
  • Travel speed (already the right curve), carrying capacity
  • Food and potion buffs (single slot), base stat growth
  • XP multipliers: fix their stacking, no curve. Attack speed stacking is fixed.

10Where to start

  1. Vocation efficiency → action time Done

    It was the only curve that could break with today's content, and the smallest change. Implemented as one pure function with no migration and no UI change.

    • computeEffectiveUnitSeconds moved to src/game/vocationStats.ts, with K = 100 as a code constant and round instead of floor.
    • The efficiency cap of 100 is removed. The floor at 0 stays.
    • Tests are in tests/vocation-speed.test.ts, and the skills page still shows only the required time.

  2. Armor and magic resist: make K tunable and level-aware

    Do this before authoring dungeons above level 1. Every monster's damage and armor will be tuned against K, and changing it afterwards means re-authoring all of them.

    1. Add a K parameter to protectionMultiplier and add armorConstant(level, curve).
    2. Resolve K at start from the content level and store it in combatSnapshot / riskConfig. A missing key means 100.
    3. Show the reduction against the dungeon's level in the planner, and "armor needed for r%" in the creature editor.
    4. Re-author monster armor on the new scale. Only the Goblin exists today.

  3. Find and quantity: knee curve

    Do this before adding more Luck or Efficiency sources, or before skill levels climb past about 150. With the knee above today's content, current players see no change.

    1. Add softCap to the curves module. calculateExpeditionRewardModifiers takes the curve as an argument.
    2. Snapshot it in hunting riskConfig, dungeon combatSnapshot, and a new nullable JSON column on gathering.
    3. Let the admin simulators pass the live config, and show the resulting bonus in the expedition UI.

11Side findings

These aren't curve problems, but they came up while tracing the pipelines and they affect balance more than any curve would.

Security · fix before any balance work

POST /api/leveling/add-multiplier, POST /api/leveling/award-xp and POST /api/test/add-item only check that the request has a signed-in session. None of them uses requireAdminApiAccess or an environment guard. Any player can grant themselves an arbitrary stackable XP multiplier, any amount of XP, or any item at any rarity. While these are open, every balance decision in this document can be bypassed. Gate them behind admin access or remove them.

Attack speed stacking · fixed

Weapons author ATTACK_SPEED as a weapon base speed: Cindermaul 0.55 (a heavy mace), Reedwind Bow 1.0. The pipeline used to add it to the character's base 1.0 and multiply it by rarity. An Epic Cindermaul at level 35 gave 2.07 attacks per round, and a Divine Wayfarer Shortblade at level 50 gave 4.05. A slow weapon still made you faster, and damage per round grew with roughly rarity squared.

Now a weapon's speed replaces the unarmed speed (weaponAttackSpeedAdjustment, src/utils/stats.ts), and rarity doesn't scale it (statScalesWithRarity, src/utils/itemInstanceStats.ts). A per-item override still can. Level growth and speed on other gear add on top as flat bonuses. The same two weapons now give 0.69 and 1.15 attacks per round.

  • Unread stats on gear: Accuracy, Lifesteal, Thorns, Mana, Mana Regen, Prayer Points, Gold Find and Experience Gain can be equipped but do nothing. Either hide them in item tooltips or mark them as inactive until they're wired in.
  • Luck's description says "Increases item drop quality" (src/types/stats.ts:376). It actually raises find chance and quantity.
  • Armor isn't shown on the character sheet (commented out at CharacterStats.tsx:233). The dungeon planner shows it raw, with no percentage.

12Decisions for you

  1. Armor target: what reduction should a full, on-level, on-tier set give? This document used about 45–48% as an illustration. It's the anchor that sets K.
  2. Where creature level comes from: the content level (dungeon or location requiredLevel, no schema change), or a new Creature.level?
  3. Vocation K: it's 100 in code. After testing with live tools, keep it, lower it to 100 − e so a specific tool keeps its old speed, or move it into /admin.
  4. Find curve: keep the early game identical with a knee, or accept a simpler plain curve that changes current numbers?
  5. Visible nerfs: is it acceptable that existing high-armor gear gets weaker in percentage terms? High-efficiency tools already did with the vocation change.
